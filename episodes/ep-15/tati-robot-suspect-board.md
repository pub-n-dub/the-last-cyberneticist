# Tati Robot Suspect Board

## Status

Working analytical memo.

Opened: `2026-08-14`

Purpose: organize the most plausible builder scenarios for the `Tati robot` using a detective-style structure:

- motive
- means
- opportunity
- evidence for
- evidence against

This is an inference document, not a proof document.

## Case Framing

The question is not simply, `Who could have built the Tati robot?`

The better question is:

Which builder or builder-group best fits the known engineering pattern, the historical context around `Simon` and `Squee`, and the visible sophistication of the unknown machine?

## Suspect 1

### `William A. Porter + Robert A. Jensen + Andrew Vall`

This is the strongest named-team hypothesis.

#### Motive

- They were already part of a machine-building culture under Berkeley.
- They had reason to continue or elaborate beyond `Simon`.
- A more creature-like robot would represent a natural escalation from a functioning educational computer into an embodied behavioral machine.

#### Means

- `Porter`: mechanical construction
- `Jensen`: electrical integration and system completion
- `Vall`: electrical integration and functional refinement

Together they already form the exact competence bundle needed for a robot that looks mechanically resolved and behaviorally intentional.

#### Opportunity

- They were active in the right time window around `Simon`
- They were working in Berkeley's orbit
- If the `Squee` period preserved workshop continuity, they are the most obvious named people to extend it

#### Evidence For

- `Simon` is explicitly documented as a team build involving these three roles
- The unknown robot appears to require a three-part competence structure very close to theirs
- The machine looks too refined for a lone amateur improvisation
- The Tati robot's finish suggests builders with prior machine experience

#### Evidence Against

- No direct source yet places all three on `Squee`, much less on the Tati robot
- No documentary connection yet ties them to Paris
- The theory is structurally persuasive but still inferential

#### Provisional Rating

`Very strong as a builder-pattern hypothesis`

`Strong as a named-builder hypothesis, but still unproven`

## Suspect 2

### `Jack Koff + one or more of Porter / Jensen / Vall`

This is the strongest hybrid-team hypothesis.

#### Motive

- Koff is directly tied to `Squee` reliability and circuitry work
- If `Squee` was a behavioral small robot and the Tati robot is a more mature behavioral creature, Koff sits in a believable transition zone

#### Means

- `Jack Koff`: known Squee troubleshooting and circuitry refinement
- plus one or more of:
  - `Porter` for mechanics
  - `Jensen` for integration
  - `Vall` for control refinement

#### Opportunity

- Koff is explicitly in Berkeley's assistant environment during the relevant period
- The existence of three hired assistants on the `Squee` side suggests a broader workshop cluster than the Simon roster alone

#### Evidence For

- Koff is the one named Squee assistant currently documented
- He is specifically associated with making Squee work reliably
- That makes him a credible bridge figure between rough prototype and finished behavioral machine

#### Evidence Against

- We do not yet know whether Koff overlapped with Porter, Jensen, or Vall on an actual shared robot project
- Koff is not yet documented as a mechanical builder on Porter's level
- This theory depends on overlap we suspect but cannot yet prove

#### Provisional Rating

`Strong transitional hypothesis`

`Especially good if Tati is treated as a Squee-descended refinement`

## Suspect 3

### `A Berkeley-adjacent workshop team whose names are only partly known`

This is the strongest overall hypothesis if one avoids overcommitting to named individuals.

#### Motive

- Berkeley's work encouraged machine-building as an educational and demonstrative activity
- A small workshop culture could naturally push beyond published or publicly famous machines
- Builders may have wanted to explore creaturely or theatrical design more deeply than Berkeley himself did

#### Means

- The required means are the same:
  - mechanical construction
  - electrical integration
  - behavioral logic

The named Simon team shows that this competence structure existed in Berkeley's orbit.

#### Opportunity

- `Edmund C. Berkeley and Associates` clearly had assistants
- `Squee` clearly involved three hired assistants
- Not all personnel in that culture are fully named in current sources

#### Evidence For

- Explains the robot without forcing unsupported direct authorship claims
- Fits the documented existence of a broader assistant environment
- Fits the possibility that a refined machine could emerge from the same culture without becoming a headline object

#### Evidence Against

- It is harder to dramatize because the theory is impersonal
- It cannot yet satisfy the desire for a single identifiable culprit

#### Provisional Rating

`Strongest overall historical explanation`

`Weaker as a dramatic culprit story, stronger as a disciplined inference`

## Suspect 4

### `Edmund C. Berkeley himself`

This is the central-name hypothesis, but not my leading one.

#### Motive

- Berkeley cared deeply about machine architecture, public demonstration, and educational systems
- He had every reason to prototype and exhibit small machines

#### Means

- Organizationally, yes
- Personally, probably through design leadership more than sole fabrication

#### Opportunity

- Directly at the center of the whole enterprise

#### Evidence For

- Everything passes through Berkeley in one way or another
- He is already associated with `Simon` and `Squee`
- The Tati robot's design grammar could plausibly descend from his thinking

#### Evidence Against

- Berkeley seems, in temperament, more oriented toward explanation and architecture than toward the most theatrical creature expression
- The refined physicality of the unknown robot feels like it may reflect stronger hands-on mechanical ambition from assistants or associates
- A direct Berkeley-only theory underestimates the documented team character of these projects

#### Provisional Rating

`Plausible as conceptual father`

`Weaker as sole physical builder`

## Suspect 5

### `A follower outside Berkeley's immediate named circle`

This is the strongest external-builder hypothesis.

#### Motive

- Berkeley's machines and publications were pedagogical
- Their design grammar could travel through plans, articles, reports, and demonstrations
- Another workshop might have taken the ideas further

#### Means

- Unknown, but feasible if the builder had access to enough fabrication and electrical knowledge

#### Opportunity

- Postwar technical cultures often spread through clubs, hobby publications, schools, demonstrations, and informal replication

#### Evidence For

- Explains why a Paris-discovered robot might exist without requiring a direct Berkeley-personnel trail
- Explains how design grammar can survive even when names are lost

#### Evidence Against

- At present, the similarity feels stronger than a loose influence case
- No clear external builder candidate has emerged yet

#### Provisional Rating

`Moderately plausible`

`Important fallback if direct-team evidence does not grow`

## Suspect 6

### `French family link through one of the named builders`

This is the romantic biographical theory.

#### Motive

- Would explain the Paris association in personal terms

#### Means

- Only meaningful if paired with a documented engineering role

#### Opportunity

- Would require travel, shipping, inheritance, or family relocation links

#### Evidence For

- At present, almost none

#### Evidence Against

- No documented French family ties found for Jensen or Vall
- No usable evidence found for Porter
- The Paris clue can be explained by many object-movement routes that do not involve family

#### Provisional Rating

`Currently weak`

## Comparative Ranking

From strongest to weakest:

1. `Berkeley-adjacent workshop culture`
2. `Porter + Jensen + Vall`
3. `Jack Koff + one or more of Porter / Jensen / Vall`
4. `Berkeley as conceptual father but not sole builder`
5. `External follower using Berkeley design grammar`
6. `French family-link explanation`

## Best Current Detective Reading

If this were a Sherlock board pinned to a wall, I would circle two statements:

### Circle 1

`The Tati robot was probably not the work of a lone genius.`

Reason:

The machine appears to demand a team ecology.

### Circle 2

`The best named suspects are Porter, Jensen, and Vall, with Jack Koff as the most important Squee-side bridge figure.`

Reason:

That is the tightest known human cluster matching the machine's implied needs.

## Most Likely Narrative

The most likely narrative at the moment is:

A small Berkeley-adjacent builder culture, already capable of moving from educational circuitry to behavioral machines, pushed beyond `Simon` and `Squee` into a more refined creature-build. Whether the exact hands were `Porter`, `Jensen`, and `Vall`, or that same competence pattern plus `Jack Koff`, the Tati robot most likely emerged from that kind of collaborative workshop rather than from isolated authorship.

## What Would Change the Rankings

Things that would raise `Porter + Jensen + Vall`:

- any direct source naming them on `Squee`
- any direct source connecting them to later small robots
- any travel / exhibition trace connecting their work to Europe

Things that would raise `Koff + others`:

- identification of the other two Squee assistants
- evidence of Koff's overlap with the Simon team
- documentation of Koff as more than a circuit troubleshooter

Things that would raise `external follower`:

- evidence of Berkeley plans circulating into a specific French or European workshop
- proof that the machine is later than the Berkeley-associates phase
