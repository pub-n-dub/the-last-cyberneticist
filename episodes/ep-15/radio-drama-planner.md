# Episode 15 Radio-Drama Planner

## Working Premise

`Logic of a Pure Mystery: The Tati Robot` is an entertaining historical mystery in the grammar of a restrained BBC-style radio drama. It does not claim to identify the robot's maker. Instead, it lets the listener enjoy the search: a strange machine, fragmentary records, competing possibilities, and a set of human voices who refuse to let uncertainty become a convenient answer.

The episode's contract with the listener is simple:

- documented material is presented as documented material;
- inference is identified as inference;
- imagined scenes provide character, atmosphere, and possible worlds—not testimony.

## Dramatic Question

`Who might have built the Tati robot, and what kind of workshop world would have made it possible?`

The answer remains open. The final movement should make that openness satisfying rather than apologetic.

## Cast

### The Host / Narrator

The present-day investigator and anchor voice. Warm, curious, and tempted by a good theory, but willing to withdraw a claim.

Function: introduces evidence, connects scenes, tells the listener which epistemic ground they are standing on.

Suggested voice treatment: natural voice; close microphone; intimate but not solemn.

### The Archivist

An exacting counterweight to the Host. The Archivist loves documents, dates, and the phrase, `That does not follow.` They are not a villain; their caution makes the drama possible.

Function: interrupts leaps of logic, reads source fragments, forces a distinction between an attractive story and a supported one.

Suggested voice treatment: subtle narrowing or light telephone/reading-room texture. Keep speech fully intelligible.

### Daniel Dennett

The finder and namer of the robot, appearing only through documented recollection where possible and otherwise through clearly announced reconstruction.

Function: supplies the Paris discovery and the machine's strange afterlife.

Suggested voice treatment: gentle age and room tone; avoid impersonation. A voiced quotation should be preceded by `Dramatized reading` unless it is a verified recording.

### Edmund C. Berkeley

The conceptual elder of the story: an advocate for mechanical intelligence, a publisher, and a nexus around which people and machines gathered.

Function: gives the listener the world of `Simon`, `Squee`, small-machine experimentation, and distributed technical work.

Suggested voice treatment: bright, precise, faintly period-radio in texture. Do not let him make claims for which no source exists.

### Jack Koff

The principal hands-on bridge from the documented `Squee` world to the hypothesized workshop culture.

Function: makes the practical work audible—wiring, fitting, testing, working away from the official center.

Suggested voice treatment: slightly younger, practical, less polished. Use only sourced words as quotation; otherwise label scenes as reconstructions.

### The Workshop Chorus

One or two versatile altered voices representing Porter, Jensen, Vall, and unnamed builders. They are not individual suspects delivering a confession.

Function: stages alternative workshop possibilities: a design problem, an argument about fabrication, a late-night test. The chorus shows the competence bundle a machine such as Tati may have required.

Suggested voice treatment: modest, distinct filters and pacing for each role. Avoid caricature; listeners must still understand the dialogue.

### The Tati Robot

No conventional dialogue. Its voice is a vocabulary of relay clicks, motor strain, contacts, a springy head movement, and silence.

Function: returns the listener to the object whenever the human story grows too confident.

Suggested voice treatment: build a recurring, recognizable sound motif rather than using generic "robot" noises.

## Voice-Changer Rules

- Preserve intelligibility before novelty; a voice effect should be audible as character, not an obstacle.
- Assign each character one stable signature: pitch range, speed, microphone distance, or room treatment. Do not stack every effect at once.
- Test the cast in headphones, a phone speaker, and at low volume.
- Avoid direct celebrity or real-person vocal imitation. Suggest period and personality through performance, not mimicry.
- Mark every reconstructed exchange in the script and recording log.

## Production Decision: Offline Character Processing

Episode 15 will be recorded first and processed afterward; it does not require live voice changing. This gives each performance time to remain clear, natural, and properly edited before a character treatment is applied.

The intended tool is a small local `Tati Radio Desk`, rather than a general-purpose voice-cloning system. It should:

- retain an untouched original take alongside every processed version;
- apply named, repeatable character presets to a recorded WAV;
- export a processed WAV plus a cue/processing log;
- support light pitch, EQ, dynamics, room treatment, and optional mechanical texture;
- never require imitation or cloning of a real person's voice.

Suggested first presets: `Host`, `Archivist`, `Berkeley`, `Koff`, `Workshop Chorus A`, `Workshop Chorus B`, and `Tati Robot`.

## Possible Structure

### Prologue: The Object

SFX: Archive room at night; paper sleeve; photograph placed on a table; a brief Tati motif.

The Host introduces an unidentified machine and the limits of the evidence. The Archivist immediately establishes the rules: discovery is not provenance; resemblance is not authorship.

### Scene 1: Paris, Recalled

SFX: Distant street, shop bell, boxes and old metal—not a literal documentary claim, but an announced sonic reconstruction.

Dennett's account introduces the named object. End on the question of what travelled with the robot and what did not.

### Scene 2: The Published Line

SFX: Typewriter, page turn, a simple relay demonstration.

Berkeley's world of `Simon` and `Squee` appears through documents and source-based readings. The Host sees lineage; the Archivist says a lineage is not yet a maker.

### Scene 3: After Hours in the Workshop

SFX: Soldering iron cooling, relay clicks, chassis screws, distant rain or ventilation.

The Workshop Chorus performs a clearly marked imagined reconstruction of the skills required: mechanical fabrication, wiring, sensing, control, and creaturely presentation. Jack Koff supplies the documented fact of work that was not confined to a formal lab.

### Scene 4: The Suspect Table

SFX: Cards set down; pencil notes; the Tati motif returns after each candidate.

The Host and Archivist examine candidate patterns: Porter/Jensen/Vall; Koff as bridge; Berkeley as conceptual center; an unnamed adjacent group; a separate French line. Each receives a short case for, case against, and an uncertainty rating.

### Scene 5: The Machine Interrupts

SFX: The most complete Tati motif so far, then a stopped mechanism.

The investigation reaches its most tempting conclusion. The Robot's sound and the Archivist's correction stop the Host from calling it solved.

### Coda: A Better Mystery

The Host concludes that the most plausible answer may be a workshop culture rather than a single genius. The final invitation is practical: find records, locate the object, reconstruct the machine, and let new evidence change the cast list.

## Sound Palette

| Dramatic space | Core sounds | Constraint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Archive | paper, photograph sleeve, lamp switch, room tone | Sparse; silence is part of the cue. |
| Paris recollection | shop bell, street wash, box lid, small wheels | Explicitly label as reconstruction. |
| Berkeley world | typewriter, relay panel, page turns | Use sound to establish work, not to fake a historical recording. |
| Workshop reconstruction | screws, solder, wire, relay/motor tests | Keep period-appropriate and mechanically plausible. |
| Tati robot | contact click, motor hum, spring, silence | Make a repeatable motif with identifiable transitions. |

## Evidence Labels for the Script

Use a visible tag in drafts and the recording log:

- `[DOCUMENTED]` — directly supported by a source.
- `[READING]` — a sourced quotation read by a performer.
- `[INFERENCE]` — a stated conclusion drawn from several clues.
- `[RECONSTRUCTION]` — invented dialogue or sound used to evoke a plausible situation.
- `[UNKNOWN]` — a gap which the episode should preserve rather than fill.

## Candidate-Scene Template

For every candidate or candidate-group, give the listener the same fair hearing:

1. What is documented?
2. What skill, connection, or chronology makes the candidate plausible?
3. What evidence is missing or contradicts the theory?
4. What would move the candidate up or down the list?

## Development Questions

- Which Dennett source or quotation can be used directly, and which parts need a reconstructed framing?
- Which source fragments best give Berkeley and Koff their own words?
- Do Porter, Jensen, and Vall need distinct speaking roles, or should they remain the Workshop Chorus to avoid false specificity?
- Can the Tati motif be built from recorded physical objects and a small electromechanical rig?
- What is the ideal running time for the prologue before the first explanatory scene?
- Which voice-change settings remain clear on ordinary phone speakers?
