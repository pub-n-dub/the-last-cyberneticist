# Episode 15 Preparation Brief

## Editorial decision

Episode 15 will be a storyboarded visual case-file, not a conventional podcast
episode or a solved-origin story. Narration and sound serve the images: the
object, documents, maps, source fragments, candidate cards, and carefully
labeled reconstructions. The visual argument must remain legible with the sound
off; the audio must not create evidence that the images cannot support.

Its central claim is deliberately modest: the Tati robot is best understood, for
now, as evidence of a collaborative Berkeley-adjacent machine culture. `Porter`,
`Jensen`, and `Vall` are documented analogues for the necessary skills; `Jack
Koff` is the documented bridge to hands-on `Squee` work. None is identified as
the robot's maker.

Working title: `Logic of a Pure Mystery: The Tati Robot`.

The production master is the storyboard. `script.md` remains a narration source
until it is divided into numbered visual beats. A spoken-only episode may be
derived later, but it is not the primary deliverable.

## Required research gate

No passage may turn an analogy, visual reading, or dramatic reconstruction into
an attribution. Before the script is locked, make a compact source sheet that
records source, precise support, quotation status, and the exact script passage
for each of these claims:

1. The robot's discovery, its Paris provenance, and the basis for calling it
   the `Tati robot`.
2. The Simon roles of Porter, Jensen, and Vall.
3. Koff's employment, offsite work on Squee, and the work he says he performed.
4. Berkeley's relation to Simon and Squee.
5. Every direct quotation, including its edition/page or stable archival link.

Keep the following as `[INFERENCE]`, even if they remain in the finished show:

- the robot's apparent maturity or theatrical intent;
- a Berkeley-adjacent workshop culture as the best explanation;
- Porter/Jensen/Vall as named builder-pattern analogues;
- Koff as a possible bridge figure;
- any claim about Berkeley's likely awareness.

Treat the French-family theory as closed unless new primary evidence emerges.

## Production preparation

- Give every board a unique ID, time range, visual description, narration,
  evidence label, source/asset status, sound cue, and transition.
- Use a consistent visible legend for `[DOCUMENTED]`, `[INFERENCE]`,
  `[RECONSTRUCTION]`, and `[UNKNOWN]`; it must be readable on a phone screen.
- Prefer the actual robot image, document facsimiles, diagrams, typography,
  maps, and photographed physical materials. Generated or illustrative imagery
  must carry a reconstruction label and cannot be used to imply provenance.
- Reserve moving-image treatment for interpretation: camera moves over actual
  documents, layered diagrams, or explicitly reconstructed workshop moments.
- Keep each claim on screen long enough to read; do not cut evidence faster than
  the viewer can assess it.
- Record the Host dry and keep every original take.
- Follow [AUDIOPLAY.md](AUDIOPLAY.md) for the cast, document-reading rules,
  recording format, and sound-byte IDs.
- Use the Archivist only to expose genuine inferential boundaries; avoid turning
  it into a repeated courtroom device.
- Label all imagined exchanges `[RECONSTRUCTION]` in the recording script and
  slate them in the session log.
- Do not imitate Dennett, Berkeley, Koff, or any other real person's voice.
- Build one repeatable Tati motif from physical/electromechanical sounds:
  contact click → low motor strain → spring/head motion → abrupt silence.
- Make a cue log with cue ID, intended duration, source/recording status,
  processing notes, and final placement.

## Definition of ready to record

- A timed, numbered visual storyboard has passed source, visual-legibility, and
  narration-readability review.
- The title and episode number agree in every document.
- All direct quotations have an accessible citation.
- Reconstruction, inference, and unknowns are marked in the session materials.
- The asset list, visual legend, sound-cue log, and a timed storyboard review
  exist.
- The ending names one concrete next action: locate provenance records or test
  the proposed machine architecture through a transparent reenactment build.
