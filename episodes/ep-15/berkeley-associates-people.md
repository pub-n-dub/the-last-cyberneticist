# Edmund C. Berkeley and Associates

## People Around `Simon` and `Squee`

Working date range: roughly `1950-1952`

This is a conservative list of the people I can currently document as working in or directly through `Edmund C. Berkeley and Associates` in the years around the building of `Simon` and `Squee`.

Important limitation:

This is **not** a complete payroll or office roster. It is a documented working list based on available historical sources. Some people are clearly named; some are only attested indirectly.

## Named and Documented

### 1. Edmund C. Berkeley

- Founder of `Edmund C. Berkeley and Associates`
- Central organizer, writer, promoter, and designer behind the educational machine program that included `Simon`, `Squee`, `Relay Moe`, and related work

Why included:

- The `Computer History Museum` pioneer biography states that in `1948` he went into business for himself as `Edmund C. Berkeley and Associates`
- The same source places `Simon` and `Squee` inside that small-robot and educational-project line

### 2. William A. Porter

- Role: `skilled mechanic`
- Specific contribution: did the `basic construction` of `Simon`

Why included:

- The `History of Information` entry on `Simon`, quoting the `Columbia University` fact sheet, explicitly names Porter as part of the build team

### 3. Robert A. Jensen

- Role: `electrical engineering graduate student` at `Columbia University`
- Specific contribution: helped engineer `Simon` into working order
- Specific work mentioned in the Columbia fact sheet: switching system, synchronization, error indication, and functional completion

Why included:

- Explicitly named in the `History of Information` entry on `Simon`, which quotes the Columbia fact sheet

### 4. Andrew Vall

- Role: `electrical engineering graduate student` at `Columbia University`
- Specific contribution: helped engineer `Simon` into working order alongside Jensen

Why included:

- Explicitly named in the `History of Information` entry on `Simon`, which quotes the Columbia fact sheet

### 5. Jack Koff

- Role: one of `three assistants Berkeley hired`
- Specific contribution: took `Squee`, which already existed in rough form, and made it work reliably by adding needed circuitry
- Background at the time: undergraduate at `City College of New York`

Why included:

- The `Computer History Museum` article `Squee: The Robot Squirrel` explicitly says `Jack Koff was one of three assistants Berkeley hired`

## Attested but Unnamed

### 6. Two additional Berkeley assistants on the `Squee` side

- Role: assistants hired by Berkeley in the `Squee` period
- Status: `unnamed in the source currently in hand`

Why included:

- The `Computer History Museum` source says `Jack Koff was one of three assistants Berkeley hired`
- That means at least `two additional assistants` existed in this period, even though their names are not provided in that source

## Best Current Roster

So the best documented list at present is:

1. `Edmund C. Berkeley`
2. `William A. Porter`
3. `Robert A. Jensen`
4. `Andrew Vall`
5. `Jack Koff`
6. `Unnamed assistant #2` on the `Squee` side
7. `Unnamed assistant #3` on the `Squee` side

## Likely Overlap

What the table tells us is this:

- `Simon` has a clearly named build team.
- `Squee` has a clearly attested assistant structure, but only one assistant currently named in the available source.
- `Berkeley` is the guaranteed overlap.
- `Porter`, `Jensen`, and `Vall` are strong candidates for overlap into the `Squee` period, but I do not yet have a source that proves each of them worked directly on `Squee`.
- `Jack Koff` is the one named `Squee` assistant we can presently place with confidence.

## Most Responsible Overlap Statement

The strongest responsible statement at this stage is:

`Simon` was built by a named Berkeley team of `Porter`, `Jensen`, and `Vall` under Berkeley's direction, while `Squee` was worked on within the same Berkeley-assistant environment and is explicitly tied to `Jack Koff` plus two additional unnamed assistants.

That means there is a high-probability `organizational overlap`, but not yet a fully documented `person-by-person overlap`.

## Provisional Overlap Hypothesis

If one were to build the most plausible overlap model without overclaiming, it would look like this:

- `Berkeley` spans both projects for certain
- `Porter / Jensen / Vall` define the named `Simon` competence bundle
- `Jack Koff` defines at least one named `Squee` technician
- the two unnamed `Squee` assistants may or may not include some of the `Simon` team, but we do not presently know

## What This List Suggests

- `Simon` was clearly a team effort rather than a one-man build
- `Squee` also involved hired technical help, not just Berkeley alone
- The named people already show the key division of labor:
  - concept / organization
  - mechanical construction
  - electrical integration
  - behavioral debugging and reliability work
