# Tati Robot Dossier

## Status

Working research memo.

Opened: `2026-08-14`

Purpose: separate what is documented from what is inferred, then use inductive logic to rank the most plausible builder scenarios for the so-called `Tati robot`.

## Scope

This dossier asks a narrow question:

Who is the most plausible mystery builder, or builder-group, behind the unidentified `Tati robot` if we take seriously the idea that it belongs to the same mechanical culture as `Squee` and `Simon`?

It also checks one specific sub-question:

Did any of the three main Squee-period engineers appear to have family ties in France that might help explain a Paris connection?

## Documented Facts

### 1. Simon was a team build, not a one-man build

The strongest documented source says `Simon` represented the combined efforts of:

- `William A. Porter`: a skilled mechanic who did the `basic construction`
- `Robert A. Jensen`: electrical-engineering graduate student
- `Andrew Vall`: electrical-engineering graduate student

The same source says Jensen and Vall took Simon when it was not yet working and engineered it so that it functioned, including switching, synchronization, and error-indication systems.

### 2. The three specialties form a complete small-machine team

Even from the limited record, the division of labor is strong:

- `Porter`: mechanical structure and fabrication
- `Jensen`: electrical integration and logic implementation
- `Vall`: electrical integration and logic implementation

This matters because the unknown robot appears to combine:

- resolved mechanical structure
- behavioral intention
- organized internal routing

### 3. Andrew Vall later had a long engineering career

The available obituary for `Andrew Emery Vall` says he graduated from Columbia, worked 43 years at Raytheon, and was known for curiosity and the ability to fix almost anything. This does not prove anything about the Tati robot, but it supports the picture of a durable engineering temperament rather than a short-lived student hobbyist.

### 4. Robert A. Jensen later had a technical-professional career

The best available biographical source says `Robert A. Jensen` was raised in the Pacific Northwest, studied at Whitman, Columbia, and Suffolk, and later practiced patent and trademark law. Again, this does not prove anything about the Tati robot, but it confirms the person exists in a documented post-Columbia trajectory.

## France Connection Check

### Result

At present, there is `no documented evidence found` that `Robert A. Jensen`, `Andrew Vall`, or `William A. Porter` had family in France.

### What was checked

- Jensen obituary and biographical references
- Vall obituary and basic family references
- broad web searches for France / Paris / family ties involving all three

### What this means

The `France angle` currently does **not** strengthen the case.

That does not refute a Paris connection for the robot itself. It only means the Paris story is not presently supported by a known family route through these three men.

### Important caution

The surname `Vall` can occur in French and broader European contexts, but surname distribution is not family proof. It is too weak to use as evidence of a specific French tie.

## Strong Inferences

These are not direct facts, but they are supported by the documented structure of the Simon team and by the visual reading of the unknown robot.

### 1. The unknown robot looks like the work of a team culture

If the robot is as mechanically resolved as it appears, then it likely required:

- a mechanical fabricator
- an electrical integrator
- a behavioral or control-minded engineer

That is exactly the kind of competence bundle already documented in the Simon team.

### 2. The unknown robot looks downstream from prior iterations

Its apparent finish, compactness, and coherence suggest:

- earlier prototypes
- earlier mistakes
- prior decisions about structure, routing, balance, and presentation

That makes it more likely to belong to an established workshop culture than to a lone improvised builder.

### 3. Berkeley himself may not have been the most likely creature-builder

From the series reading so far, Berkeley seems more attracted to:

- explanation
- demonstration
- instruction
- architecture

than to creaturely theatricality for its own sake.

This does not exclude Berkeley. But it slightly weakens the hypothesis that he personally pushed the unknown robot into its most dramatic form.

## Weak or Romantic Possibilities

These are worth preserving as imaginative leads, but they should not be treated as established.

- One of the three had hidden French family ties and this directly led to the robot turning up in Paris.
- The robot was literally a secret side-project deliberately concealed from Berkeley.
- The Paris provenance by itself proves French authorship.

None of these is currently supported by the available evidence.

## Analogy Check: Was There a "Traitorous Eight"-Style Split?

Short answer:

`No direct evidence has been found of a Fairchild-style breakaway.`

That means there is presently no documentation of:

- a collective Berkeley-associates defection
- a named internal revolt
- a rival firm founded by the assistants
- a public rupture equivalent to the `Traitorous Eight`

### What we do have instead

We do have evidence for a much softer and more interesting pattern:

- `assistant autonomy`
- `offsite build work`
- `practical machine work happening partly outside Berkeley's direct hands`

The strongest example is `Jack Koff`'s account of `Squee`:

- Berkeley hired him as one of three assistants
- Berkeley gave him the robot and told him to get it working
- Koff then took it home and worked on it in his apartment in the Bronx
- He went to Berkeley's office only about once a week
- He says most of the parts already existed, but the machine was not reliable and he added the circuitry it needed

This does **not** prove a schism.

But it does prove that Berkeley's workshop culture tolerated, and perhaps depended on, a high degree of decentralized technical labor.

### Why this matters to the Tati case

This pattern strengthens the `side-current` hypothesis:

- important machine work could happen outside the office
- assistants could exercise significant practical control over the final working form
- the published Berkeley line may not capture the full range of what was being built in that orbit

So the correct analogy is not:

`Traitorous Eight`

but rather:

`a workshop culture in which assistant-led technical work may have outrun the published official line`

That is a subtler claim, but it is better supported by the evidence we currently have.

## Inductive Builder Ranking

This ranking uses abductive reasoning: which explanation best fits the known facts, the visible form, and the social pattern of small-machine engineering in Berkeley's orbit?

### 1. Most plausible

`A Berkeley-adjacent team, with Porter/Jensen/Vall as the best living model of the required skill split`

Why this ranks first:

- We already know that exactly such a three-part team existed.
- The robot appears to require all three competencies.
- The robot looks mature enough to belong to a workshop lineage rather than a one-off improvisation.
- This hypothesis does not require direct French family ties.

This is the strongest current theory.

### 2. Plausible

`One or more of the three themselves built it, either together or in partial collaboration, after or beside the published Berkeley work`

Why this ranks second:

- The skill bundle fits extremely well.
- The robot's refinement suggests experienced hands.
- The spoken episode's suggestion of a schism or side-current fits this scenario narratively.

Why it is not ranked first:

- There is no direct documentary proof placing any of them on this specific machine.
- The Paris link remains unexplained.

### 3. Moderately plausible

`An unknown builder or team used Berkeley plans, Simon/Squee design grammar, or adjacent relay-machine traditions without being the same people`

Why this ranks third:

- Berkeley sold plans and treated these machines pedagogically.
- Design grammar can travel farther than named individuals.
- A refined machine could emerge from followers, imitators, or a related workshop.

Why it is not ranked higher:

- The current speculation is driven by unusually strong structural resemblance, not just broad stylistic similarity.

### 4. Less plausible but still possible

`Berkeley himself as primary direct builder of the Tati robot`

Why this ranks lower:

- Berkeley is clearly central to the architecture and culture.
- But the visible creatureliness and theatricality of the unknown robot seem more likely to come from a mechanically adventurous builder circle than from Berkeley alone.

This remains possible, but not presently the best fit.

### 5. Currently weak

`A specifically French family connection through Jensen, Vall, or Porter explains the robot's Paris story`

Why this ranks low:

- No supporting evidence found.
- The Paris location could be explained by many other paths: resale, exhibition, collector movement, postwar migration of objects, or a local European builder influenced by imported ideas.

## Best Current Hypothesis

The best current hypothesis is:

The `Tati robot` was most likely built by a `Berkeley-adjacent machine culture` rather than by Berkeley alone, and the strongest named candidates remain the `Porter / Jensen / Vall` trio or builders extremely close to their division of labor.

In plain terms:

- `Porter-type hands` explain the mechanical finish
- `Jensen-type hands` explain the integration
- `Vall-type hands` explain the behavior/control refinement

Whether those exact men built it is still unproven.
Whether that exact skill ecology built it is, inductively, very persuasive.

## Berkeley Awareness Scenarios

This section asks a narrower question:

If the `Tati robot` really did emerge from a Berkeley-adjacent line, would `Edmund C. Berkeley` likely have known about it?

### Scenario 1: Berkeley knew directly and chose not to publish it

This is one of the stronger scenarios.

Why it fits:

- Berkeley was at the center of the workshop culture
- He had organizational authority and broad visibility over small-machine efforts
- A machine could be real and known without becoming part of the formal published line

Why he might not publish it:

- it may not have fit the educational argument he wanted to foreground
- it may have seemed too creaturely or theatrical relative to the architecture he wanted to emphasize
- authorship may have been messy if the machine belonged mostly to assistants or associates
- it may have remained a side-build, demo object, or workshop artifact rather than a finished publication project

### Scenario 2: Berkeley knew indirectly, but only as workshop background

This is also highly plausible.

Why it fits:

- small technical groups often contain side-projects that are known socially but not formalized institutionally
- Berkeley may have known assistants were experimenting without treating the result as an official Berkeley machine
- partial awareness is easier to sustain than full public ownership

This is the scenario of:

`He knew something was being built, but did not absorb it into the official line.`

### Scenario 3: Berkeley was partly bypassed

This is possible, but weaker than the first two scenarios.

Why it still fits somewhat:

- side-projects can emerge in the margins of a workshop
- assistants may have wanted greater creaturely freedom than Berkeley's formal publications allowed

Why it is weaker:

- a highly resolved machine usually takes enough space, time, materials, and attention that total secrecy is difficult
- complete ignorance by Berkeley would be harder to maintain than partial awareness

### Scenario 4: Berkeley had no knowledge at all

This is currently the weakest awareness scenario if the machine truly came from his orbit.

Why it ranks low:

- the machine appears too substantial for pure invisibility inside a small workshop culture
- Berkeley was not a distant symbolic figure; he was an organizer of real building efforts

This scenario becomes stronger only if the robot turns out not to belong to his orbit at all.

## Awareness Ranking

From most plausible to least plausible:

1. `Berkeley knew directly and did not publish it`
2. `Berkeley knew indirectly as workshop background`
3. `Berkeley was partly bypassed`
4. `Berkeley had no knowledge at all`

## Best Current Awareness Judgment

The strongest current judgment is:

`Berkeley was probably aware at least in outline, but may not have regarded the machine as part of the publishable line he wanted attached to his name.`

That is stronger than either extreme:

- `Berkeley masterminded and concealed it`
- `Berkeley knew nothing at all`

## What Would Most Strengthen the Case

- period photographs of intermediate robots between `Squee` and the unknown robot
- builder notebooks, plans, or correspondence mentioning side projects
- evidence of travel, exhibition, sale, or export routes connecting Berkeley's circle to Paris
- stronger biographical records on Porter
- direct testimony from later family, institutional, or collector archives

## What Would Most Weaken the Case

- proof that the robot postdates the Berkeley/Squee period by too much
- proof that its internals use a design logic alien to relay-era Berkeley-style construction
- proof that the Paris origin story belongs to a wholly separate maker tradition

## Working Conclusion

The `France family connection` line is currently weak.

The `engineering-culture connection` line is currently strong.

So the Sherlock path should proceed like this:

1. Treat `Porter / Jensen / Vall` as the best named analogues for the builder profile.
2. Treat direct authorship by them as plausible but unproven.
3. Treat Berkeley-adjacent workshop lineage as the strongest overall explanation.
4. Treat the Paris story as a distribution clue, not yet an authorship clue.
