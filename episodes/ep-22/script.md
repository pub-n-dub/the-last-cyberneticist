# Episode 22: Little-Lost Robot: Final Preparations

## Script

Welcome to Episode 22 of *The Last Cyberneticist*.

Preparation ends not when every uncertainty has disappeared, but when the remaining uncertainties can be met one at a time.

In the last episode, we prepared a stable source of power and a repeatable path for programmed memory. Those are small achievements, but they change the character of the work. A machine that fails can now be questioned with fewer moving parts in the explanation.

Is the supply present and correctly connected?

Is the memory device known, programmed, and verified?

Does the processor have the clock, reset, addressing, and support hardware it needs to begin?

Can we observe the result through a serial connection, a meter, an indicator, or another stated test point?

These are not preliminaries we leave behind once the interesting work begins. They are the conditions that make the interesting work legible.

Final preparation means bringing them together without turning the bench into a maze. The power source should be stable and its connections clear. The programmed memory should have a known image and a written verification result. The processor and its support hardware should be understood well enough to identify the first expected sign of life. Tools should be ready before power is applied, not searched for after something unexpected happens.

It also means deciding in advance what we will not do.

We will not change several variables at once and call the result a diagnosis. We will not force a connector because a connection looks plausible. We will not treat an unverified ROM as a known program. We will not let a dramatic first trial obscure the condition of the machine before the trial began.

This is a cybernetic discipline in the plainest sense. Make the system observable. Make an intervention reversible where possible. Preserve a record. Let feedback change the next action.

For a living creature, preparation is mostly invisible. For a machine on a bench, it can be written down. That is an advantage. We can make a baseline: power values, installed components, ROM identity, switch positions, cable routes, initial symptoms, and the test we intend to perform. If the machine responds, we have a beginning. If it does not, we have not lost the whole day to a blank mystery.

The Little-Lost Robot is still not a promise of a finished intelligence. It is a body with a history, an arrangement of parts, and a set of possible conversations waiting to be made real. The responsible way to begin that conversation is to give every signal, intervention, and surprise a place in a trace.

Preparation has now done its work. The parts, tools, and procedures have become a starting condition.

Next comes the first integrated robot session: the point at which the machine begins to answer back.

Thank you for listening.
