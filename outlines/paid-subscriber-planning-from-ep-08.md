# Paid Subscriber Planning From Episode 8

Working planning date: `Thursday, September 3, 2026`.

## Purpose

Starting with `Episode 8`, the series has enough concrete bench detail, experimental structure, and research overflow to justify a parallel paid-subscriber layer.

The public episode should remain the clean main argument.

The paid layer should provide the things that serious listeners actually want when they decide to support the project:

- fuller bench notes
- alternate cuts or extended versions where useful
- supplements that preserve research, diagrams, and build logic
- a clearer sense that they are supporting ongoing machine work rather than only audio publication

## Editorial rule

The paid offering should not feel like:

- public episode fragments arbitrarily withheld
- filler posted only to satisfy a cadence
- generic creator-economy extras detached from the series thesis

The paid offering should feel like:

- access to the working bench
- access to the research trail
- access to version history and design decisions
- access to material too detailed, narrow, or document-like for the public mainline

## Core format starting at Episode 8

For each mainline episode from `Episode 8` onward, plan three layers:

1. `Public episode`
2. `Paid companion post`
3. `Paid archive artifact`

The distinction should stay stable so subscribers learn what they are getting.

## Layer definitions

### Public episode

This remains:

- the best single narrative version
- complete on its own
- about `24-26 minutes`
- shaped around one claim and one next step

### Paid companion post

This is the interpretive expansion that can include:

- additional bench explanation
- photographs with commentary
- wiring or signal-path explanation in prose
- false starts, constraints, and limits
- why certain design choices were rejected
- short historical side trails that would overload the public script

Target shape:

- `800-1,800 words`
- image-friendly
- readable as a standalone Substack-style post

### Paid archive artifact

This is the durable object for serious followers.

Examples:

- annotated diagrams
- wiring notes
- test procedures
- version-planning memos
- dossiers
- scan notes
- build checklists
- part-selection notes
- transcript-plus-annotations

This layer matters because it turns support into preservation.

## Why Episode 8 is the correct starting point

`Episode 8` is the first point where the series clearly generates a second body of material that is valuable but not necessary for the public argument.

Before Episode 8, the episodes are mostly doing one of two things:

- establishing the conceptual and historical frame
- making the first bench demonstrations legible

From Episode 8 onward, the work begins to produce:

- explicit machine versions
- comparator logic and evaluation states
- alternative build paths
- controller staging
- educational demonstrations
- separate research dossiers

That is exactly where a paid layer becomes structurally honest.

## Versioning rule

From `Episode 8` onward, each episode should be planned in versions before drafting is considered finished.

Use this naming pattern:

- `V1 public script`
- `V2 public script revised`
- `Paid companion`
- `Paid artifact`

When useful, add:

- `Extended bench cut`
- `Annotated transcript`
- `Photo supplement`
- `Build memo`

The important point is not bureaucracy. It is making the output legible and repeatable.

## Additions to plan immediately

Three recurring paid additions should begin now:

1. `Bench Notes`
   A recurring subscriber post that records what changed physically, what failed, and what the next experimental constraint is.

2. `Archive Packet`
   A downloadable bundle of the episode's durable artifacts: diagrams, notes, transcripts, or dossiers.

3. `Version Notes`
   A short note describing what changed between public and paid versions, or between one machine version and the next.

These three additions will make the paid offering feel coherent very quickly.

## Cadence rule

Do not promise an excessive posting schedule.

Better structure:

- one public episode
- one meaningful paid companion
- one durable paid artifact when available

Sometimes the companion and artifact can be combined. The standard should be substance, not frequency theater.

## File and folder recommendation

If we want the repo to support this cleanly, use a simple structure from `Episode 8` onward:

- `episodes/ep-XX/script.md`
- `episodes/ep-XX/paid-companion.md`
- `episodes/ep-XX/paid-artifact/`
- `episodes/ep-XX/version-notes.md`

Use `paid-artifact/` as a folder when the episode has multiple files such as images, dossiers, diagrams, or annotated notes.

## House rule for paid material

Paid additions should preserve the same voice as the public show:

- serious
- concrete
- historically aware
- technically honest
- unspectacular in a good way

Subscribers should feel they are getting closer to the work, not closer to hype.

## Immediate next step

Starting now, each episode from `Episode 8` onward should be planned with this checklist before publication:

- What is the public episode's one central claim?
- What material is too detailed for the public cut but valuable to preserve?
- Is there a companion post?
- Is there a durable artifact?
- Is there a version note explaining the difference?

If this checklist is filled in at outline stage, the paid layer will stop being an afterthought and become part of the series architecture.
