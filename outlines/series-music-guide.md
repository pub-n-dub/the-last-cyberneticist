# Series Music Guide

Reusable keyboard overhead for `The Last Cyberneticist` and the `Hero-1` series.

Current planning date: Thursday, August 6, 2026.

## Purpose

Create one small family of keyboard cues that can be recorded once and reused across multiple episodes.

The goal is not to write full songs.

The goal is to create:

- a recognizable opening identity
- a short interlude marker
- a closing figure
- one slightly more mechanical variation for the `Hero-1` arc

This keeps the series cohesive without making it sound overproduced.

## Overall sound

Target mood:

- reflective
- technical
- slightly austere
- patient
- curious

Avoid:

- bright podcast-jingle energy
- fast arpeggios
- heroic cinematic swells
- comedy stingers
- anything that sounds like news radio or a startup ad

Best keyboard sounds:

- soft electric piano
- mellow synth pad
- restrained organ-like patch
- bell-like patch used very lightly

Best production rule:

- dry to lightly reverbed
- no heavy chorus
- no dramatic stereo effects
- keep the notes intelligible

## Design rule

All cues should come from one shared motif so the show sounds unified.

Use one simple shape and derive the rest from it.

Recommended base motif in `D minor`:

- `D - A - C - F - E - D`

Why it works:

- `D` gives a grounded tonal center
- `A` implies structure without immediate closure
- `C` and `F` keep the sound reflective rather than triumphant
- `E` adds a slight unresolved lift before returning
- the return to `D` resolves quietly rather than dramatically

If `D minor` feels too dark, use `D Dorian` by letting `B natural` appear occasionally in the harmony, but keep the melody mostly the same.

## Core cue set

## Cue 1: Main intro

Use:

- opening of every episode
- opening of any trailer or short update

Length:

- `8-10 seconds`

Tempo:

- around `68-76 BPM`

Feel:

- spacious
- deliberate
- one thought at a time

Suggested performance:

Left hand:

- hold low `D` and `A`

Right hand melody:

- `D - A - C - F - E - D`

Suggested rhythm:

- each note roughly an eighth to quarter note in a slow phrase
- leave a breath after the `F`
- let the final `D` ring

Suggested form:

1. low `D` or `D-A` open fifth
2. right-hand motif once
3. optional soft repeat of the last three notes
4. stop cleanly before speech

Simple playable version:

`LH: D2 + A2 held`

`RH: D4  A4  C5  F5  E5  D5`

## Cue 2: Interlude sting

Use:

- one section break in longer episodes
- transition into a quoted reading
- break between theory and bench material

Length:

- `2-4 seconds`

Tempo:

- free or loosely in time

Feel:

- a brief marker, not a miniature song

Suggested performance:

- play only the tail of the main motif
- `C - F - E - D`

Options:

- play as four separate notes
- or play `C + F` together, then `E`, then `D`

Simple playable version:

`RH: C5  F5  E5  D5`

## Cue 3: Closing cue

Use:

- end of each episode
- end of a serious reflection

Length:

- `6-12 seconds`

Tempo:

- slightly slower than the intro

Feel:

- resolved
- reflective
- not sentimental

Suggested performance:

Left hand:

- `D` drone or open fifth `D-A`

Right hand:

- start from the middle of the motif and descend more gently
- `F - E - D - C - D`

Optional ending chord:

- `D minor`
- or open fifth `D-A` if you want more ambiguity

Simple playable version:

`LH: D2 + A2 held`

`RH: F4  E4  D4  C4  D4`

## Cue 4: Hero-1 variation

Use:

- only on Hero-1 repair and `eami` episodes
- optional low-volume bed under episode title read

Length:

- `8-12 seconds`

Tempo:

- `72-80 BPM`

Feel:

- slightly more mechanical
- slightly more process-oriented
- still restrained

How it differs from the main intro:

- add a repeated pulse in the left hand
- keep the same melodic family
- avoid turning it into a march

Suggested performance:

Left hand pulse:

- `D ... D ... A ... D`

Right hand:

- `D - A - C - F`
- short pause
- `E - D`

Optional texture:

- quietly repeat a single middle `D` between melody notes
- this gives a machine-like ticking quality

Simple playable version:

`LH: D2  D2  A2  D2`

`RH: D4  A4  C5  F5  E5  D5`

## Recording plan

Record all cues in one session.

Recommended order:

1. Main intro
2. Interlude sting
3. Closing cue
4. Hero-1 variation
5. One alternate take of each

Record each cue `3 times`:

- one neutral take
- one slightly drier and plainer take
- one slightly slower and more spacious take

That will give you enough material to choose from later without re-recording.

## Editing advice

Normalize gently, but do not crush the dynamics.

Best edit rule:

- trim handling noise
- leave a small natural tail
- fade only if the cutoff sounds abrupt

Target usage levels:

- intro under voice: low enough that consonants stay clear
- interlude: slightly louder than under-voice intro
- outro: can be a little fuller because speech is ending

## Recommended episode usage

For non-Hero episodes:

- main intro at the start
- one interlude only if the episode is long enough to need it
- closing cue at the end

For Hero-1 episodes:

- Hero-1 variation at the start
- interlude sting once between conceptual and bench sections
- closing cue at the end

## Minimal script timing

If you want a reliable repeatable structure:

1. Play the intro for `2-3 seconds` alone
2. Begin speaking over the remaining `4-6 seconds`
3. Use the interlude only at one major turn
4. Let the closing cue play alone after the final line

## What not to do

- do not write a different melody for every episode
- do not improvise long keyboard sections under speech
- do not stack too many sounds
- do not use drums at first
- do not over-score the repair material

The strength of this overhead will come from consistency and restraint.

## Fastest first version

If you want the quickest path to something usable tonight:

1. Choose one soft electric piano patch.
2. Record the main intro exactly once.
3. Record the interlude as `C - F - E - D`.
4. Record the closing cue as `F - E - D - C - D`.
5. Record the Hero-1 variation with a pulsed low `D`.

That is enough to give the whole series an audible identity.
